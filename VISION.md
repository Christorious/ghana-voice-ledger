# Ghana Voice Ledger — Vision & Purpose

> _The heart of it: a trader stands in the sun all day earning her daily bread. This tool is
> her little record-keeper — her little accountant — that quietly keeps her whole day for her,
> gives her clarity on how her business is really doing, and, over time, turns her honest effort
> into a financial identity the formal world can finally trust. Built for her, in her language,
> on the phone she owns, and owned by her._

## Who this is for

A market trader in Ghana — most often a woman — selling fish, gari, produce, cooked food from
a table in the sun, dawn to dusk. She works with her hands and her memory. She serves her
customers with her **whole attention**. She speaks Twi, Ga, Ewe, Pidgin — switching mid-sentence.
She deals in cedis and pesewas, cash and MoMo. She gives credit on trust ("pay me tomorrow") and
carries it in her head. She is sharp, capable, and running a real business — and the formal world
treats her as if she isn't there. She may not read and write easily, but she should not have to,
to be seen.

## The injustice we answer

She earns her bread the hardest way there is, yet she's locked out of what would let her grow:
no record a bank will trust, so no fair credit, no capital, no cushion for a bad week. The system
asks her to *become someone else* before it will help her, and the tools that exist impose a
foreign accounting mind on a woman who already has a sophisticated financial mind of her own. So
she stays where she is — working impossibly hard, unable to scale, one shock from going backward.

## What the tool is

**Her little accountant.** It listens to her ordinary day — her serving customers — and keeps
the record *for* her, without ever pulling her away from the customer in front of her. From that,
it gives her two things she has never had:

1. **Clarity.** What did I sell today? What did I actually make? What is my effort giving me?
   Which product moved most? Am I having a good day? — answered plainly, in her language.
2. **A key.** Over months, an honest, verifiable track record she owns and can choose to show —
   proof of a real, growing business that a bank, a susu collector, an MFI can *genuinely trust
   and genuinely back*. The end is not a neater book. The end is **access, capital, and the
   dignity of being seen** — so she can scale, hire, and develop her business.

## What it must do for her

- **Keep her day without distracting her.** It listens while she trades and records every sale
  as it happens, hands-free, so serving the customer always comes first. It knows **her voice**
  among the customers' — she is the thread it follows through the day.
- **Give her clear insight into her progress.** Daily sales and profit she can *see*; an
  appreciation of what each day's effort returns; her business's growth **day by day, week by
  week, month by month** — so her effort becomes visible to her.
- **Help her read demand.** What is in demand most; the **seasonal rhythm** of what she sells;
  a sense of **what will sell, and when that time is coming** — so she can stock and plan.
- **Inform her, not replace her judgment.** We are not her planner. We give her the information
  that lets *her* plan, and make her own business flourish and grow.
- **Build her creditworthiness honestly.** A record worth something to a lender only if it is
  hard to fake — corroborated (against MoMo), tamper-evident, and hers to reveal on her terms.

## The beliefs that make it different

1. **Meet her mind, don't replace it.** Design around how she already thinks about money — by
   customer, by time of day, good day / bad day, who owes what — not around double-entry books.
   _Congruence, not conversion._
2. **Meet her where she stands.** Her language, her cheap phone, her hands full, her eyes on the
   customer. The app should disappear into her day, not demand her attention.
3. **Honesty is the product.** The record earns a lender's trust *because it is hard to game*.
   That is both the moat and the ethic — the opposite of tools that help people look better than
   they are.
4. **It's hers.** She owns the record and chooses what to reveal, to whom. Self-sovereign. The
   tool gives *her* leverage; it never surveils her for someone else's benefit.
5. **Build with her, not extract from her.** The language, the models, the data grow from
   traders' own use, on their own phones, kept theirs — the first real Ghanaian trading-language
   technology built *for* them and *with* them, not harvested *from* them.

## Our method — Luther's principles

Named for Martin Luther, who translated for the common person by *listening to how people
actually spoke* — "look them in the mouth." We build the same way:

- **Listen to real people** — spend real time with actual sellers, in the market.
- **Accessible language** — use the terms and concepts sellers already understand; make it work
  for those who don't read or write.
- **Test by speaking** — continuously test with real users, in the real, noisy market.
- **Authentic communication** — work *with* natural seller–customer conversation, not against it.
- **Preserve the natural workflow** — never make her change how she trades to use it.
- **Practical value first** — only build what directly helps her succeed.
- **Inclusive by design** — serve the informal economy that traditional solutions ignore.

## The honest hard problems (named, not hidden)

The ambient "listening keeper" is the north star, and it is hard. We hold these openly:

- **Background noise** in a busy market.
- **Multiple speakers** — seller and customers — needing to tell *her* voice apart (speaker ID)
  and to find the sale inside the conversation.
- **Code-switching** — English + Twi/Ga/Ewe in one breath — and **local product names**.
- **Varying accents and speaking speeds.**
- Doing all of it **on a cheap phone, offline, on battery, with consent** — respecting the
  privacy of the customers around her.

We do not pretend these are solved. We ship the honest, working slice now (push-to-talk capture,
a transparent parser, a confirm/review step, a local ledger and credit book) and climb toward
the ambient dream deliberately — wake-word + short capture + end-of-day review first; speaker ID,
enhancement, and Ghanaian-language models grown from the data traders themselves contribute.

## The arc we're building toward

Day one: a record she can trust of today's sales and who owes her. Month three: a business she
can finally *see*, insight into her best days and products, and a credit book that has saved her
real money. Month twelve: a portfolio a lender trusts — and perhaps the first fair loan she has
ever been offered. Then she scales. She hires. She weathers the bad week. She is *in* the economy,
not beneath it — and so is the trader beside her.

## And how we build it — honestly, too

The way we build this is part of the vision. A tool meant to create honest financial identities
cannot itself be built on pretense: we ship what is true, we name what is not done, and we grow
it for real people, not for a demo. **The software must be as real and honest as the record it
keeps.**
