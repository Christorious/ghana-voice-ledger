# Federated Learning for an On-Device Voice Bookkeeper on Low-End Android Phones

Literature review and feasibility assessment, 2017 to September 2026. Companion to the data and feasibility memorandum in this folder.

## Evidence tags and scope

Facts marked **[V]** were read from a fetched page, in practice GitHub repositories and documentation (Flower, ONNX Runtime, ExecuTorch, LiteRT, TensorFlow examples, Google's federated-compute runtime, Apple's ml-pfl4asr, sherpa-onnx, Omnilingual ASR). Facts marked **[S]** come from search summaries of papers whose hosts this session could not open, and should be checked against the paper before citation. **[E]** marks inference. The device envelope assumed throughout is a MediaTek Helio G8x or Unisoc T6xx phone with 3 to 4 GB of memory, a background job budget near 1 GB, overnight charging at home, metered 4G data at roughly 5 to 10 cedis per gigabyte, and a user population of hundreds to low thousands of traders.

## 1. The question

The project's long-term data source is consented recordings on the trader's own phone. Federated learning promises to improve the recogniser, the keyword spotter and the extractor from those recordings without the audio ever leaving the device. The question this review answers is whether that promise holds for the phones, connectivity and population size of this project, and if not, which of its parts do.

## 2. What has been shown for speech

Federated learning for speech is real, and almost all of the evidence comes from three companies operating at a scale this project will never have. Google's production system only trains when a phone is idle, charging and on an unmetered network, which in practice means overnight on Wi-Fi, and required at least 2 GB of memory when it was described in 2019 [S]; the client runtime has since been open-sourced and now includes confidential aggregation inside trusted execution environments, while the server side remains proprietary [V]. On that infrastructure Google trained Gboard's next-word model with 1.4 million parameters over about 3,000 rounds drawing on some 1.5 million phones [S], trained production keyword spotters federatedly with false-accept and false-reject rates comparable to central training, using teacher models to supply labels because the server never sees the audio [S], and in 2024 reported the first real-world federated training of a 130-million-parameter Conformer recogniser on users' phones; the post-training error figures were not retrievable here and should be read from the paper [S]. Google's most transferable results concern learning from user corrections: filtering on-device corrections to target fresh terms gave an 8 percent relative improvement on those terms with no general regression [S], and personalising a recogniser entirely on device for proper names raised name recall from 2.4 percent to between 49 and 74 percent depending on how much the user corrected [S]. Apple built federated evaluation and tuning of on-device personalisation before it built federated training [S], and its 2025 work on differentially private federated recognition puts a price on privacy: user-level guarantees cost 1.3 to 4.6 percentage points of absolute word error, and the authors state that this requires a population of at least several million users [V]. Meta adapted a streaming recogniser on device from untranscribed in-domain audio through self-labelling and reached a 12.9 percent relative error reduction, about 70 percent of what full human supervision with central training achieved [S]. Amazon's federated noisy-student training on more than a thousand simulated clients reported a 22.5 percent relative improvement over a server-only baseline [S].

The academic record is more sobering. The Flower baseline for federated wav2vec 2.0 on TED-LIUM reaches a respectable word error rate, but each simulated client needs about 15 GB of GPU memory and a round takes 20 to 40 minutes on a high-end desktop GPU [V]. A 2021 paper titled "Federated learning in ASR: not as easy as you think" found only small gains in a realistic speaker-heterogeneous setting, mainly on the participating speakers' own data [S]. The most encouraging recent direction is parameter efficiency: federated adapters match centrally trained adapters while cutting trainable parameters by nearly 90 percent [S], and a 2026 study training only a projector and LoRA weights on a speech language model came within a fraction of a point of central training on English and within about two points on Italian [S]. No paper federates a voice activity detector, and no federated recogniser exists for code-switched African speech; the nearest code-switching work federates a switch detector for three Indian languages across two clients [S].

## 3. Whether a low-end phone can train at all

The decisive constraint is that the project's inference stack cannot learn. sherpa-onnx runs on ONNX Runtime and offers inference, hotword biasing and keyword spotting, none of which involves training [V], and Microsoft has deprecated the ONNX Runtime training packages, including the Android artefact, as of release 1.20 [V]. On-device training therefore requires a second, trainable copy of any model in another framework and a conversion path back. TensorFlow Lite's training signatures are mature but the official examples train only a dense head on a frozen backbone, and the LiteRT successor's documentation does not mention training [V]. ExecuTorch has a training extension that supports LoRA on language models, but it describes itself as experimental, runs in 32-bit floating point on the CPU only, has incomplete operator coverage and no native weight serialisation [V]. Flower's Android examples train a small convolutional network with TensorFlow Lite on ten physical phones under WorkManager [V].

The only measured speech-training costs on phones come from a 2021 study that trained a CTC recogniser with TensorFlow Lite: 18 minutes per epoch on a Snapdragon 855-class phone and 34 minutes on a Snapdragon 660-class phone, using 2.6 to 2.9 GB of memory, with the training graph split into sub-graphs to fit [S]. Those devices have faster cores and more memory than the Helio G85 or Unisoc phones traders own. On that basis, LoRA or last-layer adaptation of a 20-million-parameter streaming zipformer, whose encoder is an 85 MB float file in sherpa-onnx's releases [V], plausibly fits in half a gigabyte to a gigabyte and takes tens of minutes per local epoch over fifty short clips [E]; Omnilingual ASR at 300 million parameters occupies about 1.2 GB in 32-bit float before activations, and since ExecuTorch training is float-only it will not fit reliably in the memory a background job can obtain on a 3 GB phone [E]; on-device fine-tuning of even a small language model remains impractical, with one zeroth-order step on a 1.3-billion-parameter model taking about 1,800 seconds on a mid-range phone [S].

Communication cost is the second constraint, and the production systems avoid it entirely by requiring Wi-Fi. At 5 to 10 cedis per gigabyte, a full update of a 20-million-parameter model costs roughly half a cedi per direction per round and tens of cedis over a hundred-round schedule, whereas a LoRA adapter of a few megabytes costs a few pesewas per round and federated evaluation metrics cost effectively nothing [E]. A 2026 stress test of Flower under Africa-representative network conditions found federated training failing outright at five seconds of one-way latency, above 50 percent packet loss and at 90 percent client dropout, mostly because of transport defaults that three parameter changes fix [S]; intermittent availability also biases the global model toward the clients that are most often online unless corrected [S].

## 4. What federated learning protects and what it does not

Federated learning keeps raw audio on the device and shows the server only updates or aggregates, and federated language models have been found to memorise rare sequences less than centrally trained ones [S]. It does not make the updates innocuous. Speech content and speaker identity have been recovered from shared gradients of a keyword-spotting model [S], personalised acoustic models leak speaker identity at equal-error rates of 1 to 2 percent and gender at 96 percent purity from early layers [S], and the systematisation literature treats gradient inversion as practical rather than theoretical [S]. The standard defences are secure aggregation, which hides individual updates from the server, and user-level differential privacy, which for recognition costs 1.3 to 4.6 points of word error and needs millions of users to be meaningful [V]. With a population of hundreds to low thousands of traders, differential privacy at useful strength is out of reach without destroying the model [E].

For Ghana's Data Protection Act this matters in a specific way. A model update derived from a trader's voice is still personal data of which the project is controller, so federated learning reduces the obligations rather than removing them, and an ethics committee will ask the same questions about purpose, consent, withdrawal, security and retention that it asks of uploads, plus new ones about what an update reveals and whether aggregation is secure [E]. Keeping audio on the device and uploading only clips the trader has consented to and a reviewer has checked is simpler to explain, to audit and to withdraw from, and it is the approach Common Voice uses. Federated learning earns its complexity only where it delivers gains the donated-clip corpus cannot.

## 5. Federated learning in Africa

No published cross-device federated deployment on consumer phones in Africa was found, and none for speech. The cross-silo health studies that exist, a tuberculosis X-ray collaboration across eight countries including Ghana, a fetal ultrasound classifier across five African countries on small computers, and a malaria model on Ghanaian survey data, report that federated performance can match central training, and they list unreliable connectivity, limited infrastructure and institutional reluctance to share even updates as the obstacles [S]. Preprints on agricultural and mobile-money fraud applications make stronger claims that are not yet peer reviewed [S].

## 6. Alternatives that deliver most of the benefit

Three lighter mechanisms cover most of what the project wants from federated learning. Federated evaluation and analytics, in which each phone scores a candidate model or computes usage statistics on its own data and uploads only numbers, is what Google and Apple built first, costs bytes rather than megabytes, needs no training runtime, and answers the question the project most needs answered, namely how each model performs on real market speech [S]/[V]. Local personalisation without aggregation is the second: sherpa-onnx hotword biasing raises the probability of a trader's product names and customers' names without any retraining [V], and Google's on-device name personalisation shows how large the per-user gain from vocabulary alone can be [S]. Consented clip donation with human review, followed by central fine-tuning, is the third, and it is the baseline every federated result must beat; the literature offers no head-to-head comparison of the three against federated training for accented low-resource speech, which is itself a gap. Split learning, which sends features rather than weights, cuts communication but requires live connectivity during training and leaks speaker identity through the features [S], so it fits this setting poorly.

## 7. Federated learning and the agent

Work on federating tool-using agents, preference learning and calibration has appeared since 2024: knowledge-compendium exchange instead of weights, federated LoRA for multi-environment agents, federated preference aggregation, and federated calibration that aggregates per-client scalers [S]. Nothing federates or federatedly evaluates a voice agent's confirmation strategy or its confidence thresholds, and nothing uses confirm-and-correct signals from bookkeeping as a training signal, although Google's use of corrections and of user-feedback confidence filtering for keyword spotting shows the shape such work would take [S].

## 8. Feasibility for this application

| Component | Federate? | Reason | Expected gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voice activity, keyword and confidence thresholds | Yes, first, as federated tuning | Scalars; bytes to upload; Apple's route before training | Fewer false triggers, better confirm-or-book decisions |
| Evaluation of candidate recognisers, spotters and extractors | Yes, first, as federated evaluation | Metrics only; runs at inference cost on sherpa-onnx | Replaces guesswork about field error rates |
| Hotword and vocabulary lists | Locally only, with consented aggregate counts | Hotwords need no retraining; private frequency discovery needs millions of users | Large per-user gain on item and customer names |
| Keyword spotter acoustic model, a few million parameters | Pilot, third stage | Proven federatedly at Google and Snips; needs a TensorFlow Lite training twin | Quality comparable to central training |
| Small language or rescoring model | Pilot, third stage | Gboard recipe is mature but used millions of clients | Modest, on item and quantity phrasing |
| MiniLM extractor adapters | Later | Fits memory; labels from confirmations; ExecuTorch LoRA experimental | Unknown |
| Adapters or last layers of a 20 to 70 million parameter zipformer | Research pilot only | Memory borderline; tens of minutes per local epoch; needs pseudo-labels or corrections | 8 to 13 percent relative without labels, up to 27 percent with personal data, by the literature |
| Omnilingual ASR 300 M | No | Float-only training does not fit | Fine-tune centrally on donated clips |
| Small on-device language model | No | Minutes per training step on a mid-range phone | None |
| User-level differential privacy | No at this scale | Needs millions of users | Use consent, secure aggregation and on-device filtering instead |

The staged plan that follows begins with what is certain and defers what is speculative. First, build the baseline: consented clip donation with human review, local hotword lists and per-user threshold adaptation, and central fine-tuning of the zipformer or Omnilingual model on the donated Twi, Ga, Ewe and code-switched clips. Second, add federated evaluation and analytics as a WorkManager job that runs while charging within a data budget the user approves, computing correction rates, false-trigger counts, voice-activity statistics and confirm-or-edit rates for each candidate model and uploading aggregates of a few kilobytes. Third, add federated tuning of the scalar thresholds with simple aggregation and secure aggregation where the Android client supports it. Fourth, run a federated pilot on a tiny model, a keyword spotter or a language-model head under five million parameters with TensorFlow Lite training signatures, on 50 to 200 devices, and measure minutes, milliampere-hours, thermal throttling and megabytes per round on Helio G85 and Unisoc phones, then publish the numbers, because none exist. Only if that pilot shows gains should the fifth stage, federated adaptation of the recogniser's adapters from filtered corrections with acceptance criteria against drift, be attempted, and it should be judged against the central model from the first stage.

## 9. Gaps that a Ghanaian study could fill

No federated learning, federated evaluation or on-device training has been reported for any Ghanaian language or any African-market speech, and none for code-switched African speech. No measurement exists of on-device training time, memory, energy or thermal behaviour for any speech model on Helio G8x or Unisoc phones with 3 to 4 GB of memory. No production or academic system has run federated learning under metered mobile data with a per-user cost budget. No study measures the utility of federated learning or analytics at populations of hundreds to thousands of users, since the privacy results all assume millions. No head-to-head comparison exists among federated training, consented clip donation and local-only personalisation for accented low-resource recognition. No work federates a voice activity detector or evaluates an agent's confirmation policy federatedly. Two facts should be verified from the papers rather than from this review: the post-training error rates of Google's real-world 130-million-parameter federated recogniser, and the current state of ExecuTorch training support for quantised weights on Android.

## 10. Bottom line

Federated learning for speech works, and it has paid off only at the scale of Google, Apple and Meta, on Pixel-class phones, over Wi-Fi while charging, with labels derived from user corrections or pseudo-labels, and with privacy guarantees that presuppose millions of users. On 3 to 4 GB phones over paid 4G with a few hundred traders, the defensible path is consented and reviewed clip donation with central fine-tuning, local-only hotword and threshold personalisation, and federated evaluation and tuning to measure field quality at near-zero data cost, with true federated training confined to a small-model pilot whose time, energy and cost are measured and published. The privacy argument that makes federated learning attractive to an ethics committee, that audio never leaves the phone, is delivered just as well by local processing with consented uploads, and that is the argument to make.
