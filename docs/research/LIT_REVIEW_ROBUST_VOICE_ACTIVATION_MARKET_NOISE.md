# Robust Voice Activation in Non-Stationary Noise for a Ghanaian Market Voice Bookkeeper

Literature review, 2018 to September 2026. Companion to the data and feasibility memorandum in this folder.

## Evidence tags and a note on the current code

Facts marked **[V]** were read from a fetched page, which in this session meant GitHub repositories, wikis and documentation (Silero, TEN VAD, Picovoice, pyannote, openWakeWord, microWakeWord, sherpa-onnx, DTLN, GTCRN, DNS Challenge, PulseVAD, rVAD) and the project's own source files. Facts marked **[S]** come from search summaries of papers on hosts this session could not open and should be checked against the paper before citation. **[E]** marks inference.

The review begins with the code, because the code frames the problem. The class named `WebRTCVADProcessor` in the app does not call WebRTC. Its native-detection method returns whether the root-mean-square level of the frame exceeds a fixed integer threshold, 1,000, 800, 600 or 400 for the four aggressiveness modes, followed by a five-frame majority vote [V]. That is a fixed-threshold energy detector, the class of detector that every source below finds fails first in babble, and at the 68 to 77 dBA measured in Ghanaian markets it will fire continuously. Whichever design the project adopts, that component must be replaced.

## 1. The acoustic problem

Three independent measurement campaigns agree on the environment. Kejetia market in Kumasi averages 68 to 70 dBA in the morning, 71 to 77 dBA in the afternoon and 58 to 69 dBA in the evening, loudest on the ground floor, with loud music, advertising loudspeakers, crowds and vehicles as the sources, above Ghana's permissible commercial levels [S]. A survey of 323 traders at Kotokuraba market in Cape Coast reports 70 to 99 dBA, loudest on market-day afternoons [S]. A year-long study of 146 sites in Accra found the highest median 24-hour levels, 69 and 72 dBA, at commercial and major-road locations, with the least intermittency [S]. Conversational speech at a metre is roughly 60 to 65 dBA, so a phone held at arm's length sits at a signal-to-noise ratio between minus ten and minus five decibels before any close-talk gain [E]. Every published detector benchmark below was run at zero or ten decibels. The market lies below the operating range in which any of these systems has been shown to work always-on, which is the single most important fact in this review.

## 2. Voice activity detectors

The WebRTC detector proper is a Gaussian mixture model over log energies in six sub-bands between 80 Hz and 4 kHz with hand-written decision rules [S]. On Silero's multi-domain validation it scores an area under the curve of 0.73, falling to 0.62 on MSDWild and 0.65 on VoxConverse, against 0.97, 0.79 and 0.94 for Silero version 6 [V]. On Picovoice's open benchmark, which mixes LibriSpeech with the eighteen DEMAND environments at zero decibels, WebRTC detects half the speech at a five percent false-positive rate where Silero detects 88 percent and Picovoice's closed Cobra 99 percent [V]/[S]. Sub-band energy statistics cannot separate the trader's voice from surrounding voices with the same spectrum, and every neural detector beats it by 15 to 25 points of area under the curve on conversational, overlapped corpora.

Silero VAD is a roughly two-megabyte MIT-licensed network trained on corpora spanning thousands of languages, running in under a millisecond per 30-millisecond chunk on a single desktop thread [V]; its parameter count is reported as about 260 thousand in its own wiki and 463 thousand by an independent measurement, so treat it as unconfirmed [V]. Version 6 of August 2025 claims 16 percent fewer errors on noisy real-life data, with acknowledged weaknesses on music containing voice-like instruments and very high-pitched voices [V]. An independent test found it fires on none of a set of pure-noise windows but on 16 percent of music-only windows [V], and its own issue tracker records a regression on noise-only recordings between versions 3 and 4 that users described as concerning for always-on use [S]. No ARM latency figures are published by its authors [V]. TEN VAD, released in 2025, is far smaller at 373 to 532 kilobytes on Android, runs at 10 or 16 millisecond hops, and is the only detector with published phone figures: a real-time factor of 0.057 on a Galaxy J6+ with a Snapdragon 425 and 0.049 on an Oppo A3s with a Snapdragon 450, the closest published analogues to Helio and Unisoc phones, implying about five percent of one core [V]. Its authors show precision-recall curves above Silero and WebRTC on a vendor-curated set and claim faster speech-to-silence transitions, but publish no area under the curve or false-alarm rates [V]. NVIDIA's multilingual frame-level MarbleNet has 91.5 thousand parameters and reaches an area under the curve near 0.94 on AVA-Speech after training with real-world noise perturbation, but requires NeMo preprocessing and has no Android measurements [S]. Picovoice's Cobra is the most robust detector in its own benchmark and is closed, licensed through an access key [V]. pyannote's segmentation model is the strongest open detector of overlapped speech and is built for GPUs; even so, its overlap detector on DIHARD III misses 37 percent of overlap with 47 percent false alarms [S], which bounds what any system can hope to do with two people bargaining at once. Tiny detectors such as PulseVAD at 2 kilobytes buy their size with false alarms, 6 percent on pure noise and 33 percent on music [V], a trade the market cannot afford. Using the recogniser's own no-speech probability as the gate is the worst option of all: Whisper hallucinates on silence and noise, and the whisper.cpp project inserted Silero in front of it for that reason [S].

## 3. Detecting the trader rather than speech

The problem in a market is less whether someone is speaking than whether the trader is. Google's Personal VAD of 2019 conditions a frame-level detector on a speaker embedding from enrollment, outputs three classes, non-speech, target speech and non-target speech, and at 130 thousand parameters outperformed a cascade of a detector and a verifier [S]. Its 2022 successor is a one-megabyte quantised Conformer that works with or without enrollment, and its authors note that similar-sounding voices raise errors [S]. Neither has released weights. Target-speaker VAD from 2020 anchors per-speaker activity on i-vectors and improved dinner-party diarisation by more than 30 points, but it is an offline method for a known speaker inventory [S]. A research line at Aalborg has pushed noise robustness through self-supervised pretraining, with gains of about six points of average precision in unseen noise [S], and a September 2026 paper introduces foreground voice activity detection: an enrollment-free formulation in which the positive class is the dominant, sustained speaker, evaluated with a background false-alarm rate, and a streaming state-space implementation that its authors report beats commercial detectors and enrollment-based systems on foreground selectivity at one to two milliseconds per frame [S]. That formulation is the closest published statement of the question "which speech belongs to the trader".

The practical alternative today is a detector followed by speaker verification on the captured utterance. Speaker embedding models suitable for phones exist: CAM++ has half the parameters of ECAPA-TDNN and reaches an equal-error rate of 0.73 percent on VoxCeleb1, and sherpa-onnx ships 3D-Speaker, WeSpeaker and NeMo embeddings with Android sample apps [S]/[V]. The caveats are short segments and noise: a one-second test segment yields around 8 percent equal-error rate even with a specialised model, and one study reports ECAPA's error rising by roughly 19 points at five decibels [S]. Text-dependent verification on a fixed phrase is much stronger, with Google reporting equal-error rates near one percent on its wake phrase and a five to tenfold reduction in false accepts for under one percent extra rejects when verification is added to keyword spotting [S]. Own-voice detection through bone conduction or accelerometers, as used in earbuds and hearing aids, is the most reliable route of all and becomes available only if the trader wears the microphone [S]. No study validates an enrollment protocol for this setting; a 20 to 30 second enrollment of read numerals recorded in the market itself, matching channel and noise, is a reasonable design point without published support [E].

## 4. Wake words and numeral triggers

Wake-word systems are the one area of this literature that reports false alarms per hour, the metric that matters for an always-on device. openWakeWord, whose code is Apache-2.0 and whose pretrained models are non-commercial, targets under five percent false rejects and under 0.5 false accepts per hour on a dinner-party corpus, trains on thousands of synthesised positives and some 30,000 hours of negative speech, noise and music, and runs fifteen to twenty models in real time on one Raspberry Pi 3 core [V]. microWakeWord's official model is thresholded to at most 0.16 false accepts per hour on the same corpus [V]/[S]. Picovoice's Porcupine detects 97 percent of keywords at one false alarm per ten hours in DEMAND noise at ten decibels but supports no African language [V]/[S]. sherpa-onnx's keyword spotter is an open-vocabulary transducer that decodes only listed phrases, each with a boost and a threshold, which means a numeral-triggered mode could be added without retraining once a model with Twi-compatible units exists; none is listed today [V]. Few-shot keyword spotting from a multilingual embedding reaches an F1 of 0.75 with five examples on unseen keywords in seen languages and 0.65 in unseen languages, with a streaming false-accept rate of 4.3 percent across 22 languages [S].

A Twi wake phrase can therefore be built from tens of real recordings. The recipe that follows from openWakeWord's own method is to record twenty to fifty real utterances of the phrase from target traders, synthesise thousands of positives with the community's Twi text-to-speech voices while varying speaker, rate and pitch, mix them with Ghanaian market recordings, DEMAND square and cafeteria channels and competing Twi speech at zero to fifteen decibels with room impulse responses, train a small classifier on the frozen embedding or a five-shot head, and report false accepts per hour on a held-out hour of market audio [E]. Two precedents exist in Africa: a 2025 Kinyarwanda command set with numbers and a wake word deployed on phones and edge devices, and Mobobi's Abena AI, which answers to a Twi wake phrase and publishes no false-trigger data [S].

## 5. Enhancement front-ends help less than they promise

Small enhancers now run on phones: RNNoise at 85 kilobytes and 40 million operations per second, DTLN at under a million parameters and 2.2 milliseconds per 32-millisecond block on a Raspberry Pi 3, GTCRN at 48 thousand parameters and MIT-licensed inside sherpa-onnx, and DeepFilterNet at a real-time factor of 0.42 on a Raspberry Pi 4 [V]/[S]. The evidence that they help recognition is negative. The 2026 SAM Audio study found word error rising after separation in every configuration despite better signal quality [S], a December 2025 study found a MetricGAN+ front-end degrading four recognisers across all nine noise conditions on medical recordings by 1 to 47 points of semantic word error [S], and an earlier study of enhancement-aided detection warns that training a detector on denoised signals can lower its performance when enhancement is poor [S]. The defensible reading is to use an enhancer, if at all, only on the detection branch, to feed the recogniser the raw signal, and to settle the question for this app's own channel with an A/B test of word error on raw against enhanced market audio [E].

## 6. Noise data and an evaluation protocol for Ghanaian markets

The corpus that changes what is possible is GESMA, a Ghanaian environmental soundscape dataset from KNUST's Responsible AI Lab published in March 2026: 22,193 uncompressed recordings totalling about 62 hours, captured in situ on consumer smartphones in markets, transport hubs, schools and public spaces, with hierarchical labels and metadata for location, time of day and device [S]. Its licence and exact class list could not be read in this session and must be checked. Around it sit DEMAND's square, cafeteria and metro channels, MUSAN's music and constructed babble, WHAM!'s eighty hours of café and bar ambience, the CHiME tablet recordings on buses and streets, the DNS Challenge noise bank of some 60,000 speech-filtered clips, FSD50K and the AudioSet classes for hubbub and crowd, and open room-impulse-response sets [S]/[V]. No impulse responses for market sheds exist [E]. For competing speech, the thousand-hour Akan corpus and the Twi speech-text pairs provide realistic babble when three to eight talkers are summed [S].

A protocol follows. Build the noise bank in tiers, GESMA market and transport clips first, then DEMAND and WHAM!, then music including Ghanaian highlife and gospel recorded from loudspeakers, then generators and traffic, then overlapped Twi speech, holding out whole recording sessions rather than clips. Sweep signal-to-noise ratios from plus fifteen to minus five decibels relative to the trader's close-talk level, separately for lapel and handset channels, with reverberation times of 0.2 to 0.6 seconds. Model an eight-hour day in which the trader addresses the app perhaps two to five percent of the time, and report false triggers per market hour on the remaining audio and miss rate on the command audio at the chosen threshold, together with onset and end-pointing latency, the classical clipping and over-hang measures, the background false-alarm rate for speaker-selective systems, and processor load and battery drain per hour on a Helio G85 and a Unisoc T606 phone. The battery baseline is sobering: the only published smartphone measurement of background acoustic capture found power never falling below about 170 milliwatts with spikes to 650, which against a 5,000 milliampere-hour battery is close to one percent per hour before any model runs [S]/[E].

## 7. What comparable systems do

Commercial assistants gate listening in cascades: a tiny detector always on, a larger model only on candidates, speaker verification only on accepted candidates, and recognition only after all three, with Apple targeting about one false alarm per week [S]. Android 14 and later require a foreground service of microphone type with a persistent notification for continuous capture, forbid starting it from the background or at boot, and Transsion firmware on Tecno, Infinix and itel phones kills background work unless the user whitelists the app in its power manager [S]. Always-on listening is therefore viable only within a session the trader has started with the app on screen, and it will still be killed on some phones. sherpa-onnx bundles Silero, open-vocabulary keyword spotting, speaker verification and GTCRN enhancement under Apache-2.0 with Android samples, which makes it the natural substrate for all of the designs below [V].

## 8. What the evidence recommends, in order

The ranking favours designs that shrink the time and the set of speakers the detector must judge, because no detector has been shown to work always-on below zero decibels. First, push-to-talk with a neural end-pointer and a post-hoc speaker check: the button removes false starts, the detector only needs to find the end of the utterance and trim leading noise, TEN VAD or Silero version 6 with an end-silence of 700 to 900 milliseconds respects natural pauses, and an on-device embedding compared against a short in-market enrollment routes doubtful utterances to a confirmation screen rather than the ledger [V]/[S]/[E]. Second, a wired lapel microphone as the default hardware recommendation, since close-talk placement is the largest single gain in signal-to-noise ratio available and later permits own-voice sensing; its behaviour with cheap Transsion phones is unmeasured [E]. Third, an opt-in hands-free mode triggered by a Twi wake phrase or by spoken numerals, trained by the recipe in section 4 and gated by text-dependent speaker verification on the phrase, which the openWakeWord and microWakeWord figures suggest would produce a few tenths of a false accept per hour in dinner-party noise and several per hour in 75 dBA babble with music until measured, with a one-tap fallback kept throughout [E]. Fourth and last, the design closest to the current code, always-on detection gated by speaker identity, which pays the capture floor all day, must survive the platform restrictions, and depends on verification over one- to two-second segments at zero decibels where error rates climb toward 8 to 19 percent; if it is pursued at all it should use a foreground-selective or personal detector rather than a detector-then-verifier cascade and report the background false-alarm rate [S]/[E]. Enhancement should stay off the recogniser's path unless an A/B test on market audio shows otherwise.

## 9. Gaps and a small study that would close them

Nothing has been measured that this project needs most. No voice activity, personal-detection or wake-word benchmark exists on West African market audio, on Twi speech, or on smartphone recordings at 68 to 77 dBA. No open detector reports false alarms per hour in babble or music. No ARM or Android latency is published for Silero version 6 or the NVIDIA detector, and no battery figure exists for any neural detector or wake word on Android. No field study compares in-market enrollment with quiet-room enrollment for speaker gating. No head-to-head test exists of enhancement on the detection branch alone. Cheap wired lapel microphones on 3 to 4 GB Transsion and MediaTek phones have no measurements of gain or of Android's audio-source routing.

A study of two to three weeks in one market would move every one of these. Record about ten hours of paired handset and lapel audio from eight to ten traders across the morning, afternoon and evening bands of the Kejetia study, plus thirty-second enrollments, and annotate two hours for trader speech, other speech and non-speech. Run WebRTC, Silero version 6, TEN VAD, the NVIDIA detector, rVAD-fast and a foreground-selective or personal detector on the same audio and report area under the curve, false alarms per market hour, miss rate, clipping measures, background false-alarm rate, and processor and battery cost on a Helio G85 and a Unisoc T606. Train one Twi wake phrase and one numeral trigger with openWakeWord from synthetic positives and GESMA negatives, and measure false accepts per hour on the ten hours and recall on two hundred real triggers, with and without speaker-verification gating. Run the recogniser on raw and on GTCRN-enhanced utterances to settle the enhancement question for this channel. Publish the noise bank, labels and scripts, and the result becomes the first West African market benchmark for voice activation.
